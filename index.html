What Role Does Gender Play in Sports Conversations?

A deep dive into how gender shapes the way we talk about, cover, and experience sports.

Read the Research View Hypothesis
01 — Research Statement

Overview

Sports are a main pillar of American culture. Conversations about various sports happen in offices, on social media, in classrooms, at friends and family gatherings, and in many other social settings.

One main issue is that this conversation topic is mostly dominated by men. My project examines the role gender plays in who feels comfortable speaking about sports, who is taken seriously, and who stays silent. I am interested in both male and female stereotypes of women and men discussing sports.

Through qualitative research into young men's and women's opinions on stereotypes, feelings, and expectations in sports conversations, I have gained greater clarity on this phenomenon. My website explores how these dynamics play out and shows the data I collect in easily digestible ways.

" In what ways does gender influence how confidently and comfortably people engage in sports conversations, and what kind of stereotypes are involved with these perceptions?"

02 — Background

Research Background

01 Purpose

Purpose

First, I want to highlight an experience that many women recognize. As a woman working in sports, I often experience sexism in hurtful situations and comments. This website can provide women with validation and shared experience that this phenomenon is real and a modern issue that deserves more attention.

Second, I want to educate and inform a broader audience. I feel like most men are unaware of their bias and how their behavior affects the confidence and attitude of the surrounding women. I don't want men to feel bad, but to notice how these stereotypes of women not being into sports or knowledgeable about sports are a facade. Women are just afraid to speak up and show interest for fear of sexist backlash. Beyond men, I think some women also put this on other women, as it has become more of a cultural phenomenon. This can help women see how their actions toward other women are also sexist and harmful.

Third, I hope the project functions as a gentle call to action. I want to encourage women and men in sports communities, both online and off, to reflect on their actions and words to make sports more inclusive.

02 Audience

Audience

The stakeholders for this project include sports fans of all genders, coaches, sports media, educators, and anyone interested in gender equity and communication with or without sports knowledge.

My primary audience is women who are sports fans (particularly younger women and college-aged students who are still forming their identities within fan communities and conversations) and the men who share those spaces with them. As stated above, both groups have something to gain from exploring the outcome of this research, whether validation or acknowledgment.

Women in my primary audience probably already know this experience intimately. For them, the website offers validation and acknowledgment that this is an issue. These women probably care about being taken seriously and not questioned or dismissed.

Men in my primary audience may be encountering these ideas for the first time, though some more confident female sports fans may be as well. They value the sports community and conversations deeply. Most would describe themselves as very interested in sports and not gatekeepers. For them, the project offers a new lens to recognize patterns in their behavior that have real consequences.

03 Exigency

Exigency

This topic is worth investigating now to curb these attitudes before they become too much of a burden on women's curiosity about sports. Women's sports viewership and participation are at historic highs, yet cultural attitudes about who "really" knows sports have not kept pace with these statistics. The gap between women's engagement and their perceived credibility in sports discourse is becoming more visible and will only grow if nothing changes.

03 — Hypothesis

Working Hypothesis

Women, both those with great sports knowledge and those who are curious, are often deterred from participating in sports conversations and/or are not taken seriously when doing so. The lack of participation stems from fear of outside perspectives, as they believe people, particularly men, will doubt, question, or correct their ideas. I expect my research to reflect these ideas as I research both men's and women's experiences, expectations, and attitudes in sports conversations.
